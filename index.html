(E)Gasboard

v0.1

Gas calculations for closed incubations

(E)Gasboard turns GC or headspace measurements into gas amounts in closed incubations. It also corrects repeated sampling, calculates gas dosing, fits time-series rates and lets you explore whether gas transfer could limit a process.

Pressure, temperature, bottle volumes and gas solubility are included, with optional salinity and pH-dependent CO₂/H₂S speciation. See how the calculations work →

What this tool does

From GC peak area to bottle amount

Example: convert a CO peak area into headspace %, then calculate the amount present in the headspace, liquid and complete bottle.

From a target concentration to a gas dose

Example: calculate how much O₂ to add to reach 150 µM dissolved O₂, or how much gas is needed to reach 500 ppmv in the equilibrated headspace.

Check rates and gas-transfer capacity

Compare an observed uptake rate with literature-based or user-entered kLa and gas-property values to screen whether gas-liquid transfer could be limiting.

Batch analysis

Upload .xlsx, .csv or .tsv files. One row represents one bottle and time point, and gas columns contain GC peak areas. Supported gas headers are recognized automatically, and blank gas cells are ignored.

How (E)Gasboard works

This page shows the equations and assumptions behind Batch analysis, Single calculation, Gas dosing and the rate/mass-transfer screen. The calculations are kept visible so results can be checked and reproduced.

Res ipsa loquitur.

GC peak area → gas % → partial pressure → headspace amount → dissolved amount → total bottle amount
time-series amount → rate → Hcp + kLa → transfer capacity → transfer-limitation screen

Internal calculation units are Pa, m³, K and mol.

1. GC peak area to gas concentration

For a multipoint calibration, every calibration observation is retained, including replicates. A straight line is fitted:

A = m x + b

The sample gas concentration is then:

xsample = Asample - b m

The calibration summary reports slope, intercept, R², number of observations and calibration range. Samples outside that range are still calculated but are flagged as extrapolations.

For a multipoint calibration, the normal model includes an intercept. If that unconstrained fit would give a negative intercept - and therefore a physically impossible negative predicted peak area at 0% gas - the intercept is constrained to 0 and the slope is refitted by least squares through the origin.

With only one non-zero standard, a proportional calibration through zero is used because slope and intercept cannot both be estimated.

2. Gas concentration to partial pressure

yi = xi 100
Pabs [Pa] = Pabs [bar] × 105
pi = yi Pabs

Pressure is absolute pressure. Water vapour is neglected in v0.1, so the entered pressure is treated as the pressure of the modelled dry gas mixture. Because partial pressure changes with gas fraction and bottle pressure, it is reported per gas and time point in Results rather than as a static metadata value.

3. Headspace amount

Vheadspace = Vbottle - Vliquid
T [K] = T [°C] + 273.15
nheadspace = pi Vheadspace Z R T

v0.1 uses Z = 1 and is intended for normal incubation pressures.

4. Temperature-dependent Henry solubility

The Henry convention used here is:

H s cp = c p

with units mol m-3 Pa-1.

Reference values are stored at 298.15 K and corrected to the bottle temperature using Sander (2023):

H s cp ( T ) = H s cp,* exp [ B ( 1T - 1298.15 ) ]

Stored temperature-range metadata are used for warnings where a supporting range is available. For entries without such a range, the two-parameter correction should be regarded as a local temperature approximation.

5. Optional salting-out correction

NaCl-equivalent concentration is optional. If it is blank or 0, the pure-water Henry solubility is used. When a value is supplied, the Weisenberger-Schumpe salting-out correction is applied where a gas-specific parameter is available:

log10 ( cwater csalt ) = Ks cNaCl

This is an NaCl-equivalent approximation, not a full mixed-electrolyte model. CO has no parameter in this model, so CO is left uncorrected and flagged.

6. Dissolved amount

cdissolved = H s cp ( T ) pi
ndissolved = cdissolved Vliquid

This is the molecular dissolved gas amount. For CO₂ and H₂S, pH-dependent pools are calculated separately.

7. Optional pH-dependent speciation

CO₂ / carbonate

CO₂* ⇌ H⁺ + HCO₃⁻
HCO₃⁻ ⇌ H⁺ + CO₃²⁻

At 25 °C, the implemented values are:

  • pKa1 = 6.352
  • pKa2 = 10.329

Both are recalculated for the sample temperature.

H₂S / sulfide

H₂S ⇌ H⁺ + HS⁻
HS⁻ ⇌ H⁺ + S²⁻

At 25 °C, the implemented values are:

  • pKa1 ≈ 6.980
  • pKa2 = 14.00

H₂S, HS⁻ and S²⁻ are included. Around neutral pH the S²⁻ fraction is normally extremely small.

8. Total bottle amount

ntotal = nheadspace + nmolecular,dissolved

This definition is the same for every gas. Estimated DIC and estimated dissolved total sulfide are separate outputs.

9. Sampling-loss correction for time-series experiments

Repeated gas and liquid sampling physically removes material from a closed bottle. The measured bottle amount therefore describes what remains at that time point. When optional sampling volumes are supplied, (E)Gasboard additionally reports a sampling-corrected inventory that adds back material removed during previous sampling events.

Sampling volumes entered on row i are assumed to be removed after the measurement on that row. They do not alter the corrected value for row i; they first contribute to the correction at the next time point.

nsample,gas,i = nheadspace,i Vsample,gas,i Vheadspace,i
nsample,liquid,i = nmolecular,dissolved,i Vsample,liquid,i Vliquid,i
Nsample,<i = ∑j=1i−1 ( nsample,gas,j + nsample,liquid,j )
ncorrected,i = ntotal,bottle,i + Nsample,<i

For CO₂, a second sampling correction is calculated for total inorganic carbon when pH is available. Liquid sampling then removes the estimated DIC pool (CO₂* + HCO₃⁻ + CO₃²⁻), while headspace sampling removes gaseous CO₂. H₂S is handled analogously using the estimated dissolved total sulfide pool.

The corrected value is a mass-balance inventory, not a prediction of the exact concentration that would have occurred in an unsampled bottle. Sampling changes liquid and headspace volumes and can therefore also affect subsequent gas-liquid partitioning. Enter the actual liquid volume present at every time point; (E)Gasboard does not automatically subtract liquid_sample_mL from later rows.

CO₂: physical CO₂ and estimated DIC

CO₂ has two useful outputs and they are deliberately kept separate.

Physical CO₂

nCO₂,physical = nCO₂,headspace + nCO₂*,dissolved

This is the same physical total reported as total bottle amount.

Estimated dissolved inorganic carbon

DIC = CO₂* + HCO₃⁻ + CO₃²⁻

Estimated DIC is only added when pH is available. It does not replace the physical CO₂ result.

Important for CO₂ utilization and carbon-balance experiments: estimated DIC is strongly dependent on pH because pH determines the distribution between CO₂*, HCO₃⁻ and CO₃²⁻. Even small pH changes can substantially affect the estimated DIC pool. For quantitative CO₂ utilization or carbon-balance analyses, pH should preferably be measured at each time point rather than assumed to remain constant. Sampling-corrected DIC uses the pH entered for each sampling time point.

Detailed carbonate species remain in Extended_data.

Rate & gas-liquid mass transfer

A selected Batch interval is fitted with a straight line. Negative slopes are uptake; positive slopes are production.

n(t)=mt+b

For gas uptake, equilibrium concentration and maximum transfer capacity are:

C*= Hscppi
MTRmax= kLa VLC*

Transfer demand and the minimum kLa required for the observed uptake are:

D= ruptakeMTRmax kLa= ruptake VLC*

D = 1 is the calculated maximum transfer capacity. Values near or above 1 indicate that gas transfer may constrain the observed uptake.

Gas dosing

A dissolved target is converted to its required partial pressure. A headspace target (% or ppmv) is solved together with gas-liquid equilibrium and the pressure added by the dosing mixture.

pi= ytargetPfinal

For CO₂ and H₂S, dissolved targets can refer to the molecular gas or the estimated total dissolved pool when pH is available. If the selected dosing mixture cannot reach the target, the calculation returns an error.

Gas-phase concentration units: % and ppmv

ppmv means parts per million by volume. For the ideal gas mixtures used here, volume fraction and mole fraction are equivalent. (E)Gasboard therefore converts % and ppmv directly for headspace concentrations and dosing mixtures.

1% = 10000 ppmv

Therefore 500 ppmv = 0.05%, 1000 ppmv = 0.1%, and 10000 ppmv = 1%. ppmv is a gas-phase composition unit; it is not a mass-based ppm concentration.

Gas-transfer estimates

kLa depends strongly on vessel geometry, liquid volume, shaking conditions and medium composition. (E)Gasboard therefore uses literature-based estimates for comparable batch systems where available.

These values are intended for screening gas-transfer limitation, not as exact system-specific measurements. kLa and Henry-law values can be replaced by the user. (If you disagree or have more representative values, please let me know!)

CSV/TSV output

CSV and TSV can be downloaded as the compact Results table only, or as a ZIP data package containing Results, Extended_data, calibration tables and Rates_mass_transfer when that optional analysis has been run.

Current assumptions and scope

  • Each measurement is treated as the measured state of the bottle at that time point.
  • Optional liquid/headspace sample volumes are treated as material removed after the measurement on that row and are applied only to subsequent time points.
  • Sampling-corrected values are mass-balance inventories; actual bottle volumes must still be entered for every time point.
  • Dissolved amounts are equilibrium estimates from Henry's law.
  • Absolute pressure is used.
  • Water vapour is neglected in v0.1. A correction is planned for a later version.
  • v0.1 uses Z = 1.
  • Salting out is optional and uses NaCl-equivalent concentration.
  • Full mixed-electrolyte corrections are not included.
  • Volatile fatty acids are not included.
  • Built-in kLa values are literature-based screening estimates for shaken batch systems and can be replaced by the user.
  • The mass-transfer module currently screens gas uptake; gas-production/outgassing limitation is not yet modeled.
  • Formal uncertainty propagation is not included.
